% ABSTRACT
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
\begin{abstract}
Activation steering controls generation at inference without retraining.
In symbolic music, Sparse Activation Steering via Sparse Autoencoders enables interpretable single-layer attribute control but fails in temporal smoothing: fractional steering magnitudes fall below the Top-K sparsity threshold, zeroing the intervention.
We propose PID Steering in two forms: \emph{Spatial PID} validates control-theoretic steering in the generative music domain, while \emph{Temporal PID} dynamically adjusts $\lambda(t)$ at each autoregressive step via a closed-loop controller whose integral term accumulates error to overcome the Top-K barrier.
Experiments on the Multitrack Music Transformer show that Temporal PID overcomes the Top-K threshold failure for pitch and duration steering, enabling smooth transitions with less intervention strength and 5\% lower Fr\'{e}chet Music Distance versus static SAS.

\end{abstract}

% 1. INTRODUCTION

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Activation steering modifies internal representations at inference time to control generative outputs without retraining~\cite{turner2024activation, li2023iti, rimsky2024steering}, building on the Linear Representation Hypothesis that concepts correspond to linear directions in activation space~\cite{park2024linear, elhage2022superposition}. \citet{nguyen2026pid} proved that existing static steering methods are equivalent to proportional (P) controllers, which cannot eliminate steady-state error caused by model priors~\cite{astrom1995pid}. Symbolic music generation poses unique challenges for activation steering.

Transformer-based models such as the Multitrack Music Transformer (MMT)~\cite{dong2023multitrack} employ compact token representations unfolding over long temporal horizons, and high-level musical concepts (pitch register, rhythmic duration) lack direct vocabulary mappings. Sparse Activation Steering (SAS)~\cite{bayat2025sas} is particularly attractive for controllable symbolic music generation~\cite{dong2023multitrack, facchiano2024musicgen, panda2024steering}: by projecting activations through Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) into a higher-dimensional monosemantic feature space, it enables precise single-layer interventions free of the feature entanglement that plagues dense methods.
However, SAS imposes a strict Top-K sparsity constraint: only the $K$ largest feature activations survive re-sparsification, and all others are zeroed.
This creates a \emph{binary threshold problem} unique to sparse steering---absent in all prior dense or even adaptive methods~\cite{vogels2025ids, kang2026directer, li2026svf}---that defeats temporal smoothing.
When a cosine ramp gradually increases $\lambda$ from 0 to a target value, fractional $\lambda$ values produce feature additions too small to enter the top-$K$---the steering signal is \emph{entirely zeroed} during the ramp phase (\cref{fig:topk_threshold}), producing an abrupt binary transition instead of smooth. We propose \textbf{PID Steering} for symbolic music in two forms:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Spatial PID} applies the layer-wise PID formulation of \citet{nguyen2026pid} across the 12 sublayers of the MMT, validating control-theoretic predictions in a shallower architecture than previously studied.
    \item \textbf{Temporal PID} transposes PID control from the spatial to the temporal domain, built around a Top-K-aware error signal: the controller measures whether steered features survive re-sparsification and adapts $\lambda(t)$ accordingly at each autoregressive step. The integral term accumulates error to overcome the Top-K threshold, enabling smooth SAS steering.
\end{enumerate}

Experiments demonstrate 43--62\% less intervention and 5\% lower Fr\'{e}chet Music Distance (FMD) for PID steering versus static SAS, with smooth transitions that preserve generation quality. Code and audio examples will be publicly available upon acceptance for reproducibility.

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

\paragraph{Symbolic Music Generation.}
Symbolic music generation has been reshaped by Transformer architectures \cite{vaswani2017attention}, which excel at capturing the long-term temporal dependencies inherent to musical structures. However, traditional MIDI tokenizations often suffer from sequence length explosions \cite{fradet2023miditok}. The MMT~\cite{dong2023multitrack} is a 6-block decoder-only transformer (12 sublayers, 512 dimensions, 8 attention heads) that generates polyphonic symbolic music using a compact 6-tuple event representation (type, beat, position, pitch, duration, instrument). We use a publicly available checkpoint pre-trained on the Symbolic Orchestral Database (SOD)~\cite{crestel2018sod}, which establishes a coherent generative baseline (Pitch Class Entropy $H_0{=}2.974$, Scale Consistency $S_0{=}92.26\%$, Groove Consistency $G_0{=}93.05\%$).

\paragraph{Activation Steering.}
Dense methods---ActAdd~\cite{turner2024activation}, DiffMean~\cite{rimsky2024steering}, DirAblate~\cite{arditi2024refusal}, Mean-AcT~\cite{rodriguez2025meantransport}---compute steering vectors as the centroid difference between contrastive datasets in the residual stream and inject $\mathbf{h}^{(\ell)} \leftarrow \mathbf{h}^{(\ell)} + \alpha \cdot \mathbf{v}^{(\ell)}$ at each layer $\ell$, but suffer from feature superposition~\cite{elhage2022superposition}: in the MMT, pitch and duration vectors exhibit cosine similarity up to 0.81 (Layer~3, \cref{app:interference}), causing interference during multi-attribute steering. SAS~\cite{bayat2025sas} resolves this by training per-layer SAEs~\cite{bricken2023monosemanticity, cunningham2024sparse} to project 512-dim activations into a 4096-dim sparse space ($8{\times}$ expansion) with strict Top-K sparsity: $f(\mathbf{a}) = \text{TopK}(\text{ReLU}(\mathbf{W}_\text{enc}\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}_\text{enc}), K)$. A 16-configuration layer selection grid search identified Layer~10 as optimal for SAS---it provides maximum feature capacity ($K{=}128$) with the best monotonic response across both attributes (see \cref{app:layer_selection}). At inference, the steered activation for token $t$ at layer $\ell$ is:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\mathbf{a}}_t^\ell = \hat{\mathbf{a}}^\ell\!\left(\sigma\!\left(f(\mathbf{a}_t^\ell) + \lambda \cdot \mathbf{v}\right)\right) + \Delta
    \label{eq:sas}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma$ is Top-K ReLU re-sparsification, $\hat{\mathbf{a}}^\ell(\cdot)$ is the SAE decoder, and $\Delta := \mathbf{a}_t^\ell - \hat{\mathbf{a}}^\ell(f(\mathbf{a}_t^\ell))$ is a reconstruction correction term that preserves information lost by the autoencoder. When $\lambda < 1$, the added features $\lambda \cdot \mathbf{v}$ have insufficient magnitude to survive $\sigma$, causing the steering signal to vanish.

\paragraph{PID Control for Steering.}
\citet{nguyen2026pid} model the layer-wise forward pass as a discrete-time dynamical system and prove that static steering is a P-controller with guaranteed steady-state error. Their PID formulation computes a dynamic steering vector $\mathbf{u}(k)$ at each layer $k$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{u}(k) = K_p \mathbf{e}(k) + K_i \textstyle\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\mathbf{e}(j) + K_d (\mathbf{e}(k) - \mathbf{e}(k{-}1))
    \label{eq:pid_spatial}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{e}(k) = \boldsymbol{\mu}_\text{target}(k) - \boldsymbol{\mu}_\text{source}(k)$ is the layer-wise error signal. The integral (I) term accumulates past errors to remove residual bias; the derivative (D) term responds to the error's rate of change, damping overshoot~\cite{astrom1995pid}. Recent adaptive methods---IDS~\cite{vogels2025ids}, DIRECTER~\cite{kang2026directer}, SVF~\cite{li2026svf}, SMITIN~\cite{luo2025smitin}---operate in dense settings where attenuated signals persist. IDS constrains per-token $\alpha$ to maintain in-distribution activations; DIRECTER gates interventions via plausibility scoring; SVF learns context-dependent vector fields; Activation-LQR~\cite{nguyen2026pid} uses model-based predictive control. All assume continuous signal propagation: reducing $\alpha$ from 1.0 to 0.5 halves the steering effect but does not zero it. Under Top-K re-sparsification, this assumption breaks---$\alpha < 1$ typically zeros the \emph{entire} intervention (\cref{fig:topk_threshold}), a qualitative discontinuity that no continuous-$\alpha$ adaptation can overcome. Our temporal PID addresses this qualitatively different sparse threshold barrier by accumulating error through the integral term until $\lambda(t)$ breaches the Top-K boundary, a mechanism orthogonal to the per-token magnitude adaptation of dense methods.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

We apply PID control to symbolic music steering in two forms: \emph{Spatial PID} adapts the original layer-wise formulation~\cite{nguyen2026pid} to the MMT's dense residual stream, and \emph{Temporal PID} transposes the controller to the autoregressive time axis to solve the SAS smoothing failure.

\subsection{Spatial PID: Adapting to Symbolic Music}
\label{sec:spatial_method}

We apply \cref{eq:pid_spatial} across MMT's 12 sublayers using DiffMean vectors with an all-to-all injection strategy (layer-specific vectors applied at every sublayer), computing the steering vector sequentially. The key challenge is the MMT's shallow depth---12 layers versus 32+ in language models~\cite{nguyen2026pid}---giving the integral term fewer steps to accumulate corrections, requiring proportionally higher $K_i$ (see~\cref{sec:spatial_results}).

\subsection{Temporal PID: Solving the Sparse Threshold Problem}
\label{sec:temporal_method}

SAS interventions in the MMT occur at a single layer (Layer~10), precluding the spatial layer-to-layer PID of \cref{sec:spatial_method}. We therefore transpose the PID control variable from the \emph{spatial} domain (layer index $k$) to the \emph{temporal} domain (generation step $t$), creating a closed-loop feedback system that operates during autoregressive decoding.

\paragraph{Error Measurement.}
At each generation step $t$, we encode the current activation through the SAE and measure the mean magnitude of the top-$N$ target features (by absolute weight in the SAS vector $\mathbf{v}$):
\begin{equation}
    \bar{f}_a(t) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}|} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{T}} f(\mathbf{a}_t^\ell)_j
    \label{eq:measure}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{T}$ is the set of $N{=}32$ feature indices with largest $|v_j|$. These features serve as a ``concept fingerprint'' indicating whether the steering signal survived Top-K re-sparsification. The error signal is:
\begin{equation}
    e(t) = m^*(t) - \bar{f}_a(t)
    \label{eq:error}
\end{equation}
where $m^*(t)$ is a cosine-ramped setpoint that smoothly ramps the target activation magnitude over $T_\text{ramp}$ beats:
\begin{equation}
    m^*(t) = \begin{cases}
        \frac{m_\text{target}}{2}\left(1 - \cos\!\left(\frac{\pi \cdot t}{T_\text{ramp}}\right)\right) & t < T_\text{ramp} \\[4pt]
        m_\text{target} & t \geq T_\text{ramp}
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:setpoint}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{PID Control Law.}
The controller computes the steering magnitude at each step:
\begin{equation}
    \lambda(t) = \text{clamp}\!\Big(K_p e(t) + K_i I(t{-}1) + K_d \big(e(t) - e(t{-}1)\big)\Big)
    \label{eq:pid_temporal}
\end{equation}
where $I(t) = \text{clamp}(I(t{-}1) + e(t),\, {-}I_\text{max},\, I_\text{max})$ is the integral accumulator with anti-windup clamping~\cite{astrom1995pid}, and $\lambda(t) \in [0, \lambda_\text{max}]$. At $t{=}0$, $I(0){=}0$, $e(-1){=}0$, and $\bar{f}_a(0)$ is the unsteered activation (typically near zero for target features). The steered sparse representation becomes $\mathbf{s}(t) = f(\mathbf{a}_t^\ell) + \lambda(t) \cdot \mathbf{v}$, followed by Top-K re-sparsification and SAE decoding as in \cref{eq:sas}.

\paragraph{Intuition.}
During the ramp, $\bar{f}_a \approx 0$ creates persistent positive error that the I~term accumulates, progressively amplifying $\lambda(t)$ until the threshold is breached. The controller then settles to a \emph{just sufficient} $\lambda$, with the D~term damping overshoot at the sub-threshold--to--active transition. We chose the mean top-$N$ feature magnitude as error signal because it directly measures threshold survival.

\paragraph{Down-Steering and Directionality.}
Steering direction is encoded in the SAS vector $\mathbf{v}$, not in $\lambda(t)$. The bidirectional SAS vector (\cref{app:vector_construction}) is constructed as $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{v}^{+} - \mathbf{v}^{-}$, where positive components reinforce the target attribute and negative components suppress the opposing one. For down-steering (e.g., lowering pitch), we negate the vector: $\mathbf{v}_\text{down} = -\mathbf{v}$, so that $\lambda(t) \cdot \mathbf{v}_\text{down}$ reinforces the \emph{low} attribute features. The controller output $\lambda(t) \in [0, \lambda_\text{max}]$ remains non-negative in both directions. The error signal $e(t)$ (\cref{eq:error}) measures the magnitude of the directionally appropriate features---those corresponding to the active vector's positive components---ensuring the controller drives $\lambda(t)$ in the correct direction regardless of up/down steering. The concept fingerprint $\mathcal{T}$ is recomputed from the active (possibly negated) vector's largest absolute components.

\paragraph{Dual-Concept Control.}
For simultaneous steering, two independent controllers use Gram-Schmidt-orthogonalized SAS vectors with an expanded $2{\times}K$ budget at the steered layer (applied equally to PID and static baselines). Under the original $K$ budget, orthogonalized features from the second concept are systematically displaced by the first concept's features during Top-K selection, reducing dual success rates to $<\!50\%$ for both methods. The $2{\times}K$ expansion is therefore an SAS framework requirement for multi-attribute steering, not a PID-specific limitation; alternative SAE architectures with relaxed sparsity (e.g., RouteSAE~\cite{shi2025routesae}) may alleviate this scaling.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate on the SOD corpus using contrastive sets (1,280 samples each, \cref{app:vector_construction}) defined by the 20th/80th percentiles of pitch ($\leq$60 vs.\ $\geq$67.6 semitones) and duration ($\leq$6.5 vs.\ $\geq$14.5 ticks). SAEs are trained at each MMT layer ($512 {\to} 4096$, Top-K $K{=}128$; details in \cref{app:sae_training}). Quality degradation $\delta = |H - H_0| + \max(0, S_0 - S) + \max(0, G_0 - G)$ combines deviations from ground-truth pitch class entropy ($H_0$), scale consistency ($S_0$), and groove consistency ($G_0$); FMD uses CLaMP2 embeddings~\cite{wu2024clamp2} (details in \cref{app:metrics}). All generations use temperature 1.0, top-$k$ logit filtering (threshold 0.9), $\text{max\_seq\_len}{=}1024$, and $T_\text{ramp}{=}64$ steps.

\subsection{Spatial PID: Domain Validation}
\label{sec:spatial_results}

We validate spatial PID (\cref{eq:pid_spatial}) using DiffMean vectors across MMT's 12 sublayers ($n{=}25$, $\alpha \in \{\pm 0.5, \ldots, \pm 2.0\}$). A grid search (\cref{app:grid_search}) reveals pitch demands $8{\times}$ higher $K_i$ than duration ($0.2$ vs.\ $0.025$), reflecting stronger autoregressive priors. Error convergence replicates~\citet{nguyen2026pid}: PI/PID eliminate P-control's residual error within 12 layers. Hook ablation across 9 configurations (\cref{app:hook_ablation}) shows all\_12 are most effective, with PID reducing $\delta$ by 17--47\% versus P-only; FMD sweeps (\cref{app:fmd_sweep}) confirm improved distributional fidelity at $\alpha{=}1.0$.

\subsection{Temporal PID: Single-Concept Steering}
\label{sec:single}

\cref{tab:single} presents results with $K_p{=}1.0$, $K_d{=}0.01$, $I_\text{max}{=}10$, $\lambda_\text{max}{=}3.0$, and concept-specific $K_i$: $0.05$ for pitch, $0.025$ for duration (the $8{\times}$ ratio from \cref{app:grid_search}). For pitch-up ($n{=}40$), PID achieves 72.65~st ($\delta{=}0.45$ [0.33, 0.62]) versus static SAS 72.30 ($\delta{=}0.64$ [0.47, 0.85]) with 62\% less intervention (avg $\lambda{=}1.15$; \cref{fig:hero_trajectory}).

Negative pitch shifts $-24.0$~semitones while maintaining 98.7\% groove. Duration uses avg $\lambda \approx 1.0$---67\% less than static's $\lambda{=}3.0$. Duration-down matches static quality ($\delta{=}3.62$ vs.\ $3.37$; overlapping CIs, \cref{app:confidence_intervals}); duration-up incurs higher $\delta{=}8.45$ vs.\ $2.84$: matched-$\lambda$ analysis (\cref{app:matched_lambda}) shows static SAS at $\lambda{=}1.0$ maintains 91.3\% scale vs.\ PID's 84.7\%, indicating within-sequence $\lambda$ variation amplifies degradation for this attribute. PID's activations evolve smoothly ($\text{std}(\Delta \bar{f}_a) < 0.003$; \cref{app:smoothness}) versus static SAS's binary step.

The concept fingerprint is robust to $|\mathcal{T}|$ (range 0.37~st across $N \in \{8,\!16,\!32,\!64\}$; \cref{app:fingerprint}), and PID outperforms step-function and minimal-$\lambda$ baselines (\cref{app:threshold_baselines}). Gain sweeps (\cref{app:gain_robustness}) confirm stability across $2{\times}$ perturbations (pitch 72.8--75.1~st); $T_\text{ramp}$ sensitivity (\cref{app:tramp_sensitivity}) identifies a sweet spot at $\{32, 64\}$ steps.

\paragraph{FMD Analysis.}
PID achieves 5.3\% lower FMD than static SAS for pitch (\cref{tab:single}), as dynamic $\lambda(t)$ avoids over-steering early tokens. Setpoint sweeps show $m_\text{target}{=}2.0$ is optimal for dual steering. \paragraph{Component Ablation.}
\label{sec:ablation}
P-only control yields $\lambda_\text{avg}{=}0.664$---too conservative for the Top-K threshold. \subsection{Round-Trip Steering}
\label{sec:roundtrip}

A unique capability of temporal PID is \emph{reversible steering}: steer away from a conditioned prefix, hold, then steer back toward original characteristics---impossible with static SAS, which applies a fixed $\lambda$ throughout. We evaluate this with an asymmetric three-phase schedule: Phase~1 (96~tokens, steer away, $m{=}0.75$), Phase~2 (64~tokens, hold with linear $\lambda$ decay to 50\%), Phase~3 (192~tokens, steer back, $m{=}1.25$), using 16-beat conditioned prefixes from extreme songs ($n{=}20$ per scenario). We include a \emph{release} baseline that steers away and holds but stops steering entirely in Phase~3 ($\lambda{=}0$), isolating the contribution of active back-steering. Full results are in \cref{app:roundtrip}.

For pitch, the high$\to$down$\to$up scenario shows the strongest result: PID recovers to 67.8~st, dramatically closer to the unsteered baseline (81.2~st) than static one-way steering (39.6~st) or passive release (59.0~st), reducing recovery error by $69\%$. For low$\to$up$\to$down, PID reduces error by $27\%$ (11.4 vs.\ 15.7~st). Duration exhibits even larger gains: $84\%$ lower recovery error for low$\to$up$\to$down (3.9 vs.\ 24.7~ticks). Crucially, the release comparison confirms that recovery is actively driven---not merely passive relaxation---with PID consistently outperforming release across all four scenarios.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced PID Steering for symbolic music: \emph{Spatial PID} validates control-theoretic steering~\cite{nguyen2026pid} in a shallow architecture, while \emph{Temporal PID} overcomes SAS's Top-K threshold failure via integral error accumulation, enabling smooth sparse steering with 43--62\% less intervention strength and 5\% lower FMD for pitch, while concept-specific gain tuning---motivated by the $8{\times}$ $K_i$ asymmetry between pitch and duration---ensures quality preservation for attributes where the underlying SAS vectors are well-behaved. Round-trip steering (\cref{sec:roundtrip}) demonstrates a unique capability of temporal PID: reversible multi-phase control trajectories that static methods cannot express, with 27--84\% better attribute recovery across pitch and duration, and active back-steering consistently outperforming passive release. Gain robustness sweeps confirm stability across $2{\times}$ perturbations, while matched-$\lambda$ analysis (\cref{app:matched_lambda}) identifies trajectory-induced degradation for duration-up as a direction for future work.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
% Our evaluation uses a single model (MMT) and dataset (SOD); the $8{\times}$ $K_i$ asymmetry may reflect model-specific priors rather than universal properties. Duration-up degrades scale consistency due to both the SAS vector and PID's dynamic trajectory (\cref{app:matched_lambda}). Sample sizes ($n{=}40$) are modest and perceptual validation is absent. Future work includes adaptive gain scheduling, plausibility-gated methods~\cite{kang2026directer}, and SAE variants like RouteSAE~\cite{shi2025routesae}.

Our evaluation uses a single model (MMT) and dataset (SOD); gain portability across architectures remains untested. We hypothesize that the $8{\times}$ $K_i$ asymmetry primarily reflects MMT's learned priors rather than universal musical properties, though validating this requires cross-architecture studies. Duration-up steering degrades scale consistency; matched-$\lambda$ analysis suggests both the SAS vector and PID's dynamic trajectory contribute (\cref{app:matched_lambda}). We tested piecewise-constant $\lambda$ scheduling---holding $\lambda(t)$ fixed for $N \in \{4, 8, 16\}$ steps between PID updates---but this did not improve scale consistency (84--86\% across all intervals vs.\ 92\% for static SAS; \cref{app:piecewise}), indicating that the degradation stems from the adaptive feedback dynamics themselves rather than per-token $\lambda$ jitter. Sample sizes ($n{=}40$) are modest and perceptual validation is absent. Anti-windup clamping ($I_\text{max}$) and $\lambda_\text{max}$ bounds provide practical stability, though formal OOD guarantees are lacking. Future work includes adaptive gain scheduling, integration with plausibility-gated methods~\cite{kang2026directer}, cross-architecture validation, and SAE variants such as RouteSAE~\cite{shi2025routesae} that may reduce Top-K threshold sensitivity.

\section*{Impact Statement}
This work advances controllable music generation. Activation steering techniques carry dual-use risks in broader settings; in symbolic music, the primary concern is unauthorized style imitation, which we mitigate by releasing only the method, not artist-specific vectors.

\section{Steering Vector Construction}
\label{app:vector_construction}

\paragraph{DiffMean Vectors.}
For each layer $\ell$, we intercept the summary activation $\mathbf{h}$ of the last fully contextualized token during a forward pass over contrastive sets. The steering vector is the centroid difference:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{v}^{(\ell)} = \frac{1}{N_+}\sum_{i=1}^{N_+}\mathbf{h}^{(\ell)}_{+,i} - \frac{1}{N_-}\sum_{j=1}^{N_-}\mathbf{h}^{(\ell)}_{-,j}
\end{equation}
where $N_+ = N_- = 1{,}280$ are samples from the ``High'' and ``Low'' contrastive pools extracted from the SOD corpus using the 20th/80th percentiles: average pitch ($\leq$60 vs.\ $\geq$67.6 semitones) and average duration ($\leq$6.5 vs.\ $\geq$14.5 ticks). These thresholds were validated to be stable across alternative extreme quantile choices.

\paragraph{SAS Vectors.}
Given sparse encodings of the contrastive sets ($S^+$ and $S^-$), behavior-specific features are isolated via frequency filtering with threshold $\tau{=}0.08$ (requiring a minimum 8\% activation frequency). Shared Feature Removal zeroes features active in both pools, yielding a bidirectional vector $\mathbf{v}^{(b,\ell)} = \mathbf{v}^{+} - \mathbf{v}^{-}$ that reinforces target features while suppressing opposing ones.

\section{Layer Selection for SAS}
\label{app:layer_selection}

A 16-configuration grid search evaluated SAS injection at individual layers and layer groups, balancing steering effectiveness against quality degradation~$\delta$. Key findings:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Multi-layer SAS broadcasting causes catastrophic failure}: unlike DiffMean, applying SAS vectors across multiple layers cascades SAE reconstruction noise and Top-K re-sparsification errors, producing total quality collapse.
    \item \textbf{Layer~10 is optimal}: under the Adaptive~K strategy (K scaling from 32 at Layer~0 to 128 at Layers~8--11), Layer~10 provides maximum feature capacity for abstract musical representations while retaining sufficient depth to propagate overrides before output generation.
    \item Layer~10 achieves the best monotonic response curve across both pitch and duration, with a 48$\times$ reduction in intervention footprint versus DiffMean (128 features vs.\ $512{\times}12$ dimensions).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Adaptive K Strategy.}
SAE sparsity scales linearly with depth: $K{=}32$ (Layer~0), $K{=}64$ (Layers~1--3), $K{=}96$ (Layers~4--7), $K{=}128$ (Layers~8--11). This accommodates increasing representational complexity in deeper layers.

\section{SAE Training Details}
\label{app:sae_training}

One SAE is trained per transformer layer. Architecture: $512 \to 4096$ ($8{\times}$ expansion), tied weights ($\mathbf{W}_\text{dec} = \mathbf{W}_\text{enc}^\top$), per-layer zero-mean unit-variance normalization. Training data: 640K activation vectors extracted via forward hooks from 10K tracks uniformly sampled from the SOD. Optimizer: Adam, LR $= 10^{-4}$. Loss:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L} = \text{MSE}(\mathbf{a}, \hat{\mathbf{a}}) + \lambda_{L_1} \cdot \mathbb{E}[|f(\mathbf{a})|], \quad \lambda_{L_1} = 10^{-3}
\end{equation}

\section{Feature Interference Analysis}
\label{app:interference}

In the dense residual stream, pitch and duration DiffMean vectors exhibit an average absolute cosine similarity of \textbf{0.49} across all 12 layers, peaking at \textbf{0.81 in Layer~3}. This severe entanglement causes destructive interference during multi-attribute steering, requiring geometric decoupling via GSO ($\mathbf{v}^{\perp}_d = \mathbf{v}_d - \text{proj}_{\mathbf{v}_p}\mathbf{v}_d$).

In the sparse SAE space, pitch and duration vectors have an average cosine similarity of $-0.27$ (anti-correlated) with 51.8\% shared feature overlap (958/4096 features), ranging from 26.5\% at Layer~6 to 75.0\% at Layer~3. For dual SAS steering, we apply Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization in sparse space and expand the Top-K budget to $2{\times}K$ to prevent feature competition.

\section{Steering Metrics}
\label{app:metrics}

\paragraph{Quality Degradation.}
$\delta = |H - H_0| + \max(0, S_0 - S) + \max(0, G_0 - G)$ is the cumulative deviation from ground-truth SOD corpus statistics using MusPy metrics where $H$ is Pitch Class Entropy, $S$ is Scale Consistency, $G$ is Groove Consistency, and subscript 0 denotes ground-truth SOD values. The asymmetric penalty reflects musical priors: increases in scale/groove consistency above the corpus mean are benign, whereas decreases indicate structural degradation. Entropy uses absolute deviation since both extremes (uniform randomness and single-note collapse) are undesirable.

\paragraph{Steering Success.}
A generation is successful if its mean target attribute shifts in the specified direction relative to the unsteered baseline (unconditioned) or the conditioning prefix (conditioned).

\paragraph{Fr\'{e}chet Music Distance.}
FMD is computed using CLaMP2~\cite{wu2024clamp2} embeddings with MLE Gaussian estimation over 4,474 SOD reference MIDIs, following~\citet{wu2024fmd}.

\section{Runtime Overhead}
\label{app:runtime}

Temporal PID's per-token overhead relative to static SAS consists of three lightweight operations: (1)~computing the error signal $e(t)$ via a mean over $N{=}32$ pre-indexed feature activations (negligible), (2)~the PID control law---three scalar multiply-adds plus two clamping operations (\cref{eq:pid_temporal}), and (3)~updating the integral accumulator $I(t)$. Both static SAS and temporal PID share the dominant cost: SAE encoding ($512 {\to} 4096$), Top-K re-sparsification, and SAE decoding ($4096 {\to} 512$) at the steered layer, plus the reconstruction correction $\Delta$.

We benchmark per-token wall-clock time over $n{=}20$ samples (512 max tokens, single A10G GPU). Unsteered baseline generation runs at $9.11 \pm 0.02$~ms/token. Static SAS adds the shared SAE encode/decode pass, increasing cost to $9.50 \pm 0.67$~ms/token ($+4.3\%$). Temporal PID reaches $9.68 \pm 0.60$~ms/token ($+6.3\%$ vs.\ baseline), with the PID controller itself contributing only $+1.9\%$ marginal overhead beyond static SAS. In practice, autoregressive sampling and attention dominate: the PID-specific computation adds $\mathcal{O}(N + 1)$ scalar operations per token atop the $\mathcal{O}(d_\text{sparse})$ SAE pass ($d_\text{sparse}{=}4096$), making temporal adaptive control essentially free relative to static steering.
