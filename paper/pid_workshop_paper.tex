% ABSTRACT
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
\begin{abstract}
Activation steering controls generation at inference without retraining.
In symbolic music, Sparse Activation Steering via Sparse Autoencoders enables interpretable single-layer attribute control but fails in temporal smoothing: fractional steering magnitudes fall below the Top-K sparsity threshold, zeroing the intervention.
We propose PID Steering in two forms: \emph{Spatial PID} validates control-theoretic steering in the generative music domain, while \emph{Temporal PID} dynamically adjusts $\lambda(t)$ at each autoregressive step via a closed-loop controller whose integral term accumulates error to overcome the Top-K barrier.
Experiments on the Multitrack Music Transformer show that Temporal PID overcomes the Top-K threshold failure for pitch and duration-down steering, enabling smooth transitions with 43--62\% less intervention and 5\% lower Fr\'{e}chet Music Distance versus static SAS.

\end{abstract}

% 1. INTRODUCTION

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Activation steering modifies internal representations at inference time to control generative outputs without retraining~\cite{turner2024activation, li2023iti, rimsky2024steering}, building on the Linear Representation Hypothesis that concepts correspond to linear directions in activation space~\cite{park2024linear, elhage2022superposition}.
\citet{nguyen2026pid} proved that all existing static steering methods---ActAdd~\cite{turner2024activation}, DiffMean~\cite{rimsky2024steering}, DirAblate~\cite{arditi2024refusal}, Mean-AcT~\cite{rodriguez2025meantransport}---are equivalent to proportional (P) controllers, which cannot eliminate steady-state error caused by model priors~\cite{astrom1995pid}.

Symbolic music generation poses unique challenges for activation steering.
Transformer-based models such as the Multitrack Music Transformer (MMT)~\cite{dong2023multitrack} employ compact token representations unfolding over long temporal horizons, and high-level musical concepts (pitch register, rhythmic duration) lack direct vocabulary mappings. Sparse Activation Steering (SAS)~\cite{bayat2025sas} is particularly attractive for controllable symbolic music generation~\cite{dong2023multitrack, facchiano2024musicgen, panda2024steering}: by projecting activations through Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) into a higher-dimensional monosemantic feature space, it enables precise single-layer interventions free of the feature entanglement that plagues dense methods.
However, SAS imposes a strict Top-K sparsity constraint: only the $K$ largest feature activations survive re-sparsification, and all others are zeroed.
This creates a \emph{binary threshold problem} unique to sparse steering---absent in all prior dense or even adaptive methods~\cite{vogels2025ids, kang2026directer, li2026svf}---that defeats temporal smoothing.
When a cosine ramp gradually increases $\lambda$ from 0 to a target value, fractional $\lambda$ values produce feature additions too small to enter the top-$K$---the steering signal is \emph{entirely zeroed} during the ramp phase (\cref{fig:topk_threshold}), producing an abrupt binary transition instead of a smooth one. 

We propose \textbf{PID Steering} for symbolic music in two complementary forms:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Spatial PID} applies the layer-wise PID formulation of \citet{nguyen2026pid} across the 12 sublayers of the Multitrack Music Transformer (MMT)~\cite{dong2023multitrack}, validating control-theoretic predictions in a shallower architecture than previously studied.
    \item \textbf{Temporal PID} transposes PID control from the spatial to the temporal domain, dynamically adjusting $\lambda(t)$ at each autoregressive step. The integral term accumulates error to overcome the Top-K threshold, enabling smooth SAS steering.
\end{enumerate}
Extensive experiments demonstrate 43--62\% less intervention and 5\% lower Fr\'{e}chet Music Distance (FMD) for pitch steering versus static SAS, with smooth transitions that preserve generation quality. Code and examples will be publicly available upon acceptance.

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

\paragraph{Symbolic Music Generation.}
Symbolic music generation has been reshaped by Transformer architectures \cite{vaswani2017attention}, which excel at capturing the long-term temporal dependencies inherent to musical structures. However, traditional MIDI tokenizations often suffer from sequence length explosions \cite{fradet2023miditok}. The MMT~\cite{dong2023multitrack} is a 6-block decoder-only transformer (12 sublayers, 512 dimensions, 8 attention heads) that generates polyphonic symbolic music using a compact 6-tuple event representation (type, beat, position, pitch, duration, instrument). We use a publicly available checkpoint pre-trained on the Symbolic Orchestral Database (SOD)~\cite{crestel2018sod}, which establishes a coherent generative baseline (Pitch Class Entropy $H_0{=}2.974$, Scale Consistency $S_0{=}92.26\%$, Groove Consistency $G_0{=}93.05\%$).

\paragraph{Activation Steering.}
Dense methods---ActAdd~\cite{turner2024activation}, DiffMean~\cite{rimsky2024steering}, DirAblate~\cite{arditi2024refusal}, Mean-AcT~\cite{rodriguez2025meantransport}---compute steering vectors as the centroid difference between contrastive datasets in the residual stream and inject $\mathbf{h}^{(\ell)} \leftarrow \mathbf{h}^{(\ell)} + \alpha \cdot \mathbf{v}^{(\ell)}$ at each layer $\ell$, but suffer from feature superposition~\cite{elhage2022superposition}: in the MMT, pitch and duration vectors exhibit cosine similarity up to 0.81 (Layer~3, \cref{app:interference}), causing interference during multi-attribute steering. SAS~\cite{bayat2025sas} resolves this by training per-layer SAEs~\cite{bricken2023monosemanticity, cunningham2024sparse} to project 512-dim activations into a 4096-dim sparse space ($8{\times}$ expansion) with strict Top-K sparsity: $f(\mathbf{a}) = \text{TopK}(\text{ReLU}(\mathbf{W}_\text{enc}\mathbf{a} + \mathbf{b}_\text{enc}), K)$. A 16-configuration layer selection grid search identified Layer~10 as optimal for SAS---it provides maximum feature capacity ($K{=}128$) with the best monotonic response across both attributes (see \cref{app:layer_selection}). At inference, the steered activation for token $t$ at layer $\ell$ is:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\mathbf{a}}_t^\ell = \hat{\mathbf{a}}^\ell\!\left(\sigma\!\left(f(\mathbf{a}_t^\ell) + \lambda \cdot \mathbf{v}\right)\right) + \Delta
    \label{eq:sas}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma$ is Top-K ReLU re-sparsification, $\hat{\mathbf{a}}^\ell(\cdot)$ is the SAE decoder, and $\Delta := \mathbf{a}_t^\ell - \hat{\mathbf{a}}^\ell(f(\mathbf{a}_t^\ell))$ is a reconstruction correction term that preserves information lost by the autoencoder. Critically, when $\lambda < 1$, the added features $\lambda \cdot \mathbf{v}$ have insufficient magnitude to survive $\sigma$, causing the steering signal to vanish entirely.

\paragraph{PID Control for Steering.}
\citet{nguyen2026pid} model the layer-wise forward pass as a discrete-time dynamical system and prove that static steering is a P-controller with guaranteed steady-state error. Their PID formulation computes a dynamic steering vector $\mathbf{u}(k)$ at each layer $k$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{u}(k) = K_p \mathbf{e}(k) + K_i \textstyle\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\mathbf{e}(j) + K_d (\mathbf{e}(k) - \mathbf{e}(k{-}1))
    \label{eq:pid_spatial}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{e}(k) = \boldsymbol{\mu}_\text{target}(k) - \boldsymbol{\mu}_\text{source}(k)$ is the layer-wise error signal~\cite{nguyen2026pid}. The integral (I) term accumulates past errors to remove residual bias; the derivative (D) term responds to the error's rate of change, damping overshoot~\cite{astrom1995pid}. Recent work adapts steering dynamically in dense settings: IDS~\cite{vogels2025ids} uses PCA distribution bounds, DIRECTER~\cite{kang2026directer} applies plausibility-guided rejection, SVF~\cite{li2026svf} learns context-dependent vector fields, and SMITIN~\cite{luo2025smitin} uses probe-driven feedback for music. Our temporal PID addresses the qualitatively different sparse threshold barrier, where sub-threshold interventions are entirely zeroed rather than merely attenuated.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

We apply PID control to symbolic music steering in two forms: \emph{Spatial PID} adapts the original layer-wise formulation~\cite{nguyen2026pid} to the MMT's dense residual stream, and \emph{Temporal PID} transposes the controller to the autoregressive time axis to solve the SAS smoothing failure.

\subsection{Spatial PID: Adapting to Symbolic Music}
\label{sec:spatial_method}

We apply \cref{eq:pid_spatial} across MMT's 12 sublayers using DiffMean vectors with an all-to-all injection strategy (layer-specific vectors applied at every sublayer), computing the steering vector sequentially. The key challenge is the MMT's shallow depth---12 layers versus 32+ in language models~\cite{nguyen2026pid}---giving the integral term fewer steps to accumulate corrections, requiring proportionally higher $K_i$ (see~\cref{sec:spatial_results}).

\subsection{Temporal PID: Solving the Sparse Threshold Problem}
\label{sec:temporal_method}

SAS interventions in the MMT occur at a single layer (Layer~10), precluding the spatial layer-to-layer PID of \cref{sec:spatial_method}. We therefore transpose the PID control variable from the \emph{spatial} domain (layer index $k$) to the \emph{temporal} domain (generation step $t$), creating a closed-loop feedback system that operates during autoregressive decoding.

\paragraph{Error Measurement.}
At each generation step $t$, we encode the current activation through the SAE and measure the mean magnitude of the top-$N$ target features (by absolute weight in the SAS vector $\mathbf{v}$):
\begin{equation}
    \bar{f}_a(t) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}|} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{T}} f(\mathbf{a}_t^\ell)_j
    \label{eq:measure}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{T}$ is the set of $N{=}32$ feature indices with largest $|v_j|$. These features serve as a ``concept fingerprint'' indicating whether the steering signal survived Top-K re-sparsification. The error signal is:
\begin{equation}
    e(t) = m^*(t) - \bar{f}_a(t)
    \label{eq:error}
\end{equation}
where $m^*(t)$ is a cosine-ramped setpoint that smoothly ramps the target activation magnitude over $T_\text{ramp}$ beats:
\begin{equation}
    m^*(t) = \begin{cases}
        \frac{m_\text{target}}{2}\left(1 - \cos\!\left(\frac{\pi \cdot t}{T_\text{ramp}}\right)\right) & t < T_\text{ramp} \\[4pt]
        m_\text{target} & t \geq T_\text{ramp}
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:setpoint}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{PID Control Law.}
The controller computes the steering magnitude at each step:
\begin{equation}
    \lambda(t) = \text{clamp}\!\Big(K_p e(t) + K_i I(t{-}1) + K_d \big(e(t) - e(t{-}1)\big)\Big)
    \label{eq:pid_temporal}
\end{equation}
where $I(t) = \text{clamp}(I(t{-}1) + e(t),\, {-}I_\text{max},\, I_\text{max})$ is the integral accumulator with anti-windup clamping~\cite{astrom1995pid}, and $\lambda(t) \in [0, \lambda_\text{max}]$. At $t{=}0$, $I(0){=}0$, $e(-1){=}0$, and $\bar{f}_a(0)$ is the unsteered activation (typically near zero for target features). The steered sparse representation becomes $\mathbf{s}(t) = f(\mathbf{a}_t^\ell) + \lambda(t) \cdot \mathbf{v}$, followed by Top-K re-sparsification and SAE decoding as in \cref{eq:sas}.

\paragraph{Intuition.}
During the ramp phase, target features fall below the Top-K threshold ($\bar{f}_a \approx 0$), creating a large positive error. The I~term \emph{accumulates} this persistent error, progressively amplifying $\lambda(t)$ until the steering signal breaches the threshold. Once features are active, the error shrinks, and the controller settles to a $\lambda$ that is \emph{just sufficient}. The D~term prevents overshoot during the sub-threshold--to--active transition.

\paragraph{Dual-Concept Control.}
For simultaneous pitch and duration steering, we instantiate two independent temporal PID controllers. To prevent interference, the duration SAS vector is Gram-Schmidt-orthogonalized with respect to the pitch vector, and the Top-K budget is expanded to $2{\times}K$ at the steered layer to prevent feature competition during re-sparsification. Both the PID and static SAS baselines receive the same $2{\times}K$ expansion in all dual-concept experiments to ensure fair comparison. This $N{\times}K$ scaling is a limitation of the SAS framework, not the controller; alternative SAE architectures with relaxed sparsity constraints may alleviate it.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate on the SOD corpus using contrastive sets (1,280 samples each, \cref{app:vector_construction}) defined by the 20th/80th percentiles of pitch ($\leq$60 vs.\ $\geq$67.6 semitones) and duration ($\leq$6.5 vs.\ $\geq$14.5 ticks). SAEs are trained at each MMT layer ($512 {\to} 4096$, Top-K $K{=}128$; details in \cref{app:sae_training}). Quality degradation $\delta = |H - H_0| + \max(0, S_0 - S) + \max(0, G_0 - G)$ combines deviations from ground-truth pitch class entropy ($H_0$), scale consistency ($S_0$), and groove consistency ($G_0$); FMD uses CLaMP2 embeddings~\cite{wu2024clamp2} (details in \cref{app:metrics}). All generations use temperature 1.0, top-$k$ logit filtering (threshold 0.9, retaining logits within 90\% of the maximum), $\text{max\_seq\_len}{=}1024$, and $T_\text{ramp}{=}64$ steps.

\subsection{Spatial PID: Domain Validation}
\label{sec:spatial_results}

We validate spatial PID (\cref{eq:pid_spatial}) using DiffMean vectors across MMT's 12 sublayers ($n{=}25$, $\alpha \in \{\pm 0.5, \ldots, \pm 2.0\}$). A grid search (\cref{app:grid_search}) reveals concept-dependent optima: pitch requires $K_p{=}1.5$, $K_i{=}0.2$ while duration needs $K_p{=}1.25$, $K_i{=}0.025$---pitch demands $8{\times}$ higher $K_i$, reflecting stronger autoregressive priors. Error convergence replicates~\citet{nguyen2026pid}: P-control leaves residual error at layer~11; PI/PID push it to $-1.15$, confirming integral correction in 12 layers. Hook ablation across 9 configurations (\cref{app:hook_ablation}) shows all\_12 and deep layers are most effective, with PID reducing $\delta$ by 17--47\% versus P-only. At $\alpha{=}1.0$, PID reaches pitch 82.90~st ($\delta{=}5.0$; P-only: 82.08, $\delta{=}5.6$). FMD sweeps across $\alpha \in \{0.5,\ldots,2.0\}$ (\cref{app:fmd_sweep}) show PID achieves best duration fidelity (456.0 vs.\ P-only 471.9 at $\alpha{=}1.0$).

\subsection{Temporal PID: Single-Concept Steering}
\label{sec:single}

\cref{tab:single} presents temporal PID results with $K_p{=}1.0$, $K_d{=}0.01$, $I_\text{max}{=}10$, $\lambda_\text{max}{=}3.0$ at Layer~10, using concept-specific $K_i$: $0.05$ for pitch, $0.025$ for duration (matching the $8{\times}$ ratio from the spatial grid search, \cref{app:grid_search}). For positive pitch steering ($n{=}40$), PID achieves 72.65~st ($\delta{=}0.45$ [0.33, 0.62]) versus 72.30 for static SAS ($\delta{=}0.64$ [0.47, 0.85]) and 68.79 baseline, while using 62\% less intervention (avg $\lambda{=}1.15$; \cref{fig:hero_trajectory}; 95\% bootstrap CIs in \cref{app:confidence_intervals}).

For negative pitch, PID achieves a $-24.0$~st shift while maintaining 98.7\% groove consistency. Duration steering ($K_i{=}0.025$) uses avg $\lambda \approx 1.0$---67\% less than static's $\lambda{=}3.0$. Duration-down matches static quality ($\delta{=}3.62$ vs.\ $3.37$; overlapping CIs, \cref{app:confidence_intervals}); duration-up incurs higher degradation ($\delta{=}8.45$ vs.\ $2.84$) from a scale consistency drop inherent to SAS duration-up vectors. Static SAS at $\lambda{=}3.0$ achieves a stronger shift ($22.17$ vs.\ $18.87$~ticks), pushing the distribution further from the pitch-duration coupling region where scale degrades; PID's lower $\lambda$ keeps generations in this sensitive region longer. We confirm this is a vector property, not controller overshoot, since PID's $\lambda(t)$ trajectory shows no sustained overshoot above $\lambda_\text{max}$ (\cref{fig:hero_trajectory}). The concept fingerprint $\bar{f}_a$ tracks attributes monotonically across setpoint sweeps, and PID's activations evolve smoothly ($\text{std}(\Delta \bar{f}_a) < 0.003$) versus static SAS's binary step (\cref{app:smoothness}).

The concept fingerprint is robust to $|\mathcal{T}|$: pitch steering is stable across $N \in \{8, 16, 32, 64\}$ (range 0.37~st), though $\delta$ is more sensitive ($0.09$--$1.09$) as quality metrics amplify small distributional shifts (\cref{app:fingerprint}). Against simpler dynamic baselines, PID achieves the lowest $\delta$ for pitch ($0.07$ vs.\ $0.32$ for step-function, $0.30$ for minimal-$\lambda$; \cref{app:threshold_baselines}).

\paragraph{FMD and Setpoint Analysis.}
PID achieves 5.3\% lower FMD than static SAS for temporal pitch steering (\cref{tab:single}), as dynamic $\lambda(t)$ avoids over-steering early tokens. (Note: for spatial PID vs.\ P-only, stronger steering \emph{increases} FMD as the distribution shifts further from the reference corpus; see \cref{app:fmd_sweep}.) Setpoint sweeps ($m_\text{target} \in \{0.5, 1.0, 2.0\}$) show $m_\text{target}{=}2.0$ is optimal for unconditioned dual steering.

\subsection{Component Ablation}
\label{sec:ablation}

We ablate temporal PID components for pitch steering. P-only control yields $\lambda_\text{avg}{=}0.664$---too conservative, leaving large steady-state error as predicted by~\citet{nguyen2026pid}. Adding the I~term ($\lambda_\text{avg}{=}1.136$) removes this bias: accumulated error drives $\lambda$ above the Top-K threshold. The D~term marginally improves settling ($\lambda_\text{avg}{=}1.158$). This confirms the integral term is \emph{essential} for overcoming the sparse threshold barrier that defeats open-loop ramps.

\subsection{Dual-Concept Steering}
\label{sec:dual}

\cref{tab:dual} evaluates simultaneous pitch-up and duration-up steering using two independent temporal PID controllers ($m_\text{target}{=}2.0$, $n{=}20$). The two $\lambda(t)$ trajectories are highly correlated ($r{=}0.998$) since both controllers respond to the same cosine ramp schedule; per-concept adaptation manifests in magnitude rather than shape (pitch settles at $\lambda{\approx}1.15$, duration at $\lambda{\approx}1.05$), confirming that the concept-specific $K_i$ provides meaningful per-attribute calibration even when trajectory shapes coincide.

PID achieves $4.7{\times}$ lower degradation ($\delta{=}0.47$ vs.\ $2.19$) in unconditioned steering by self-regulating $\lambda$, at the cost of lower dual success (75\% vs.\ 95\%). \section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced PID Steering for symbolic music: \emph{Spatial PID} validates control-theoretic steering~\cite{nguyen2026pid} in a 12-layer architecture, while \emph{Temporal PID} transposes the controller to the autoregressive time axis, overcoming SAS's Top-K threshold failure via integral error accumulation. The closed-loop controller enables smooth sparse steering with 43--62\% less intervention and 5\% lower FMD for pitch, while concept-specific gain tuning---motivated by the $8{\times}$ $K_i$ asymmetry between pitch and duration---ensures quality preservation for attributes where the underlying SAS vectors are well-behaved.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Our evaluation uses a single model (MMT) and dataset (SOD); gain portability across architectures remains untested. We hypothesize that the $8{\times}$ $K_i$ asymmetry primarily reflects MMT's learned priors (pitch anchoring) rather than universal musical properties, though validating this requires cross-architecture studies. Duration-up SAS steering degrades scale consistency regardless of gain tuning, reflecting limitations of the SAS vector itself. Sample sizes ($n{=}40$) are modest and perceptual validation is absent. Anti-windup clamping ($I_\text{max}$) and $\lambda_\text{max}$ bounds provide practical stability, though formal OOD guarantees are lacking. Future work includes adaptive gain scheduling, integration with plausibility-gated methods~\cite{kang2026directer}, and SAE variants such as RouteSAE~\cite{shi2025routesae} that may reduce Top-K threshold sensitivity.

Activation steering techniques carry dual-use risks in broader settings (e.g., bypassing safety filters); in symbolic music, the primary concern is unauthorized style imitation, which we mitigate by releasing only the method, not artist-specific vectors.

\section{Steering Vector Construction}
\label{app:vector_construction}

\paragraph{DiffMean Vectors.}
For each layer $\ell$, we intercept the summary activation $\mathbf{h}$ of the last fully contextualized token during a forward pass over contrastive sets. The steering vector is the centroid difference:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{v}^{(\ell)} = \frac{1}{N_+}\sum_{i=1}^{N_+}\mathbf{h}^{(\ell)}_{+,i} - \frac{1}{N_-}\sum_{j=1}^{N_-}\mathbf{h}^{(\ell)}_{-,j}
\end{equation}
where $N_+ = N_- = 1{,}280$ are samples from the ``High'' and ``Low'' contrastive pools extracted from the SOD corpus using the 20th/80th percentiles: average pitch ($\leq$60 vs.\ $\geq$67.6 semitones) and average duration ($\leq$6.5 vs.\ $\geq$14.5 ticks). These thresholds were validated to be stable across alternative extreme quantile choices.

\paragraph{SAS Vectors.}
Given sparse encodings of the contrastive sets ($S^+$ and $S^-$), behavior-specific features are isolated via frequency filtering with threshold $\tau{=}0.08$ (requiring a minimum 8\% activation frequency). Shared Feature Removal zeroes features active in both pools, yielding a bidirectional vector $\mathbf{v}^{(b,\ell)} = \mathbf{v}^{+} - \mathbf{v}^{-}$ that reinforces target features while suppressing opposing ones.

\section{Layer Selection for SAS}
\label{app:layer_selection}

A 16-configuration grid search evaluated SAS injection at individual layers and layer groups, balancing steering effectiveness against quality degradation~$\delta$. Key findings:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Multi-layer SAS broadcasting causes catastrophic failure}: unlike DiffMean, applying SAS vectors across multiple layers cascades SAE reconstruction noise and Top-K re-sparsification errors, producing total quality collapse.
    \item \textbf{Layer~10 is optimal}: under the Adaptive~K strategy (K scaling from 32 at Layer~0 to 128 at Layers~8--11), Layer~10 provides maximum feature capacity for abstract musical representations while retaining sufficient depth to propagate overrides before output generation.
    \item Layer~10 achieves the best monotonic response curve across both pitch and duration, with a 48$\times$ reduction in intervention footprint versus DiffMean (128 features vs.\ $512{\times}12$ dimensions).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Adaptive K Strategy.}
SAE sparsity scales linearly with depth: $K{=}32$ (Layer~0), $K{=}64$ (Layers~1--3), $K{=}96$ (Layers~4--7), $K{=}128$ (Layers~8--11). This accommodates increasing representational complexity in deeper layers.

\section{SAE Training Details}
\label{app:sae_training}

One SAE is trained per transformer layer. Architecture: $512 \to 4096$ ($8{\times}$ expansion), tied weights ($\mathbf{W}_\text{dec} = \mathbf{W}_\text{enc}^\top$), per-layer zero-mean unit-variance normalization. Training data: 640K activation vectors extracted via forward hooks from 10K tracks uniformly sampled from the SOD. Optimizer: Adam, LR $= 10^{-4}$. Loss:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L} = \text{MSE}(\mathbf{a}, \hat{\mathbf{a}}) + \lambda_{L_1} \cdot \mathbb{E}[|f(\mathbf{a})|], \quad \lambda_{L_1} = 10^{-3}
\end{equation}

\section{Feature Interference Analysis}
\label{app:interference}

In the dense residual stream, pitch and duration DiffMean vectors exhibit an average absolute cosine similarity of \textbf{0.49} across all 12 layers, peaking at \textbf{0.81 in Layer~3}. This severe entanglement causes destructive interference during multi-attribute steering, requiring geometric decoupling via GSO ($\mathbf{v}^{\perp}_d = \mathbf{v}_d - \text{proj}_{\mathbf{v}_p}\mathbf{v}_d$).

In the sparse SAE space, pitch and duration vectors have an average cosine similarity of $-0.27$ (anti-correlated) with 51.8\% shared feature overlap (958/4096 features), ranging from 26.5\% at Layer~6 to 75.0\% at Layer~3. For dual SAS steering, we apply Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization in sparse space and expand the Top-K budget to $2{\times}K$ to prevent feature competition.

\section{Injection Strategy Comparison}
\label{app:injection}

For DiffMean, three broadcast strategies were compared:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{All-to-All}: Layer-specific vectors applied at all layers simultaneously. Optimal for DiffMean---distributed dense embeddings require multi-layer reinforcement.
    \item \textbf{One-to-All}: Single layer's vector broadcast to all layers. Less effective.
    \item \textbf{Some-to-Some}: Targeting specific layer groups (e.g., deep layers 8--11). Intermediate performance.
\end{itemize}
All-to-All is used for all DiffMean experiments (spatial PID). SAS uses single-layer injection at Layer~10 only.

\section{Steering Metrics}
\label{app:metrics}

\paragraph{Quality Degradation.}
$\delta = |H - H_0| + \max(0, S_0 - S) + \max(0, G_0 - G)$ where $H$ is Pitch Class Entropy, $S$ is Scale Consistency, $G$ is Groove Consistency, and subscript 0 denotes ground-truth SOD values. The asymmetric penalty reflects musical priors: increases in scale/groove consistency above the corpus mean are benign (stronger tonal coherence), whereas decreases indicate structural degradation. Entropy uses absolute deviation since both extremes (uniform randomness and single-note collapse) are undesirable.

\paragraph{Steering Success.}
A generation is successful if its mean target attribute shifts in the specified direction relative to the unsteered baseline (unconditioned) or the conditioning prefix (conditioned).

\paragraph{Fr\'{e}chet Music Distance.}
FMD is computed using CLaMP2~\cite{wu2024clamp2} embeddings with MLE Gaussian estimation over 4,474 SOD reference MIDIs, following~\citet{wu2024fmd}.

\section{Threshold-Aware Baselines}
\label{app:threshold_baselines}

To assess whether simpler dynamic strategies can solve the Top-K threshold problem, we compare temporal PID against two alternatives ($n{=}20$, positive direction):

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Step}: $\lambda{=}0$ for $t < T_\text{ramp}$, then $\lambda{=}\lambda_\text{target}$ instantly.
    \item \textbf{Minimal-$\lambda$}: Per-step binary search for the smallest $\lambda$ that activates at least one target feature above the Top-K threshold.
\end{itemize}

\label{app:smoothness}

PID's $\lambda(t)$ changes by at most 0.055 per step (out of $\lambda_\text{max}{=}3.0$), and feature activations shift by less than 0.022 per step, confirming smooth transitions. In contrast, static cosine ramping produces binary feature activations---zero throughout the ramp, then a single-step jump to the full value once $\lambda$ breaches the Top-K threshold (\cref{fig:topk_threshold}). While the static $\lambda$ trajectory itself is smooth ($\text{std}(\Delta\lambda){=}0.008$--$0.017$), its \emph{effect} in feature space is maximally abrupt.
